The Cost of Staying in a Comfortable IT Role for Five More Years

Before staying in the same IT role for another five years, compare what the job preserves with what it may postpone.

Use realistic scenarios, not an imagined salary against your actual paycheck. Your current schedule, stability, and development opportunities have value, while an outside move brings uncertainty.

You don't need to decide immediately. Identify the responsibilities you'd gain by staying, investigate credible alternatives, and set conditions that would make a move worthwhile. That turns staying into an informed choice rather than a decision renewed automatically each year.

Separate a hypothetical pay gap from a forecast

Suppose two hypothetical paths differ by $10,000 a year in base pay. Over five years, that simple difference is $50,000 before taxes, raises, benefits, and other changes.

That arithmetic illustrates why a recurring difference matters. It does not establish that you could obtain the higher salary or that switching would be better overall.

Use actual opportunities when available. Compare guaranteed and variable pay separately, and consider benefits, working hours, travel, and stability.

A salary screenshot is not enough information to calculate the cost of your own decision.

Write down the capabilities staying would help you develop

Ask what is likely to change about your responsibility:

Possible development Evidence to look for
More difficult problems A defined opportunity to investigate or own them
Project experience A realistic assignment with support and review
Deeper specialty Responsibilities that let you apply new knowledge
Mostly unchanged work Familiar duties repeating without a specific growth opportunity

A comfortable role can still be developmental. A busy role can offer little new learning. Exhaustion doesn't distinguish the two.

Write the capabilities you expect to demonstrate in a year, then discuss the opportunities needed to develop them.

If the list remains vague, don't assume tenure will create those responsibilities. Seek specifics before including future growth as a reason to remain in the current role.

Put the value of your current conditions on the page

Remote work, a supportive manager, predictable hours, and time for family can be worth preserving.

Do not dismiss those conditions because a new title looks better. A move that increases pay while removing the autonomy you value may be a poor trade.

The purpose of the review is not to pressure you into leaving. It is to make the benefits of staying explicit enough that you can compare them honestly with the benefits of moving.

A deliberate decision to stay is different from never examining the alternative.

Run a small market test before making a large commitment

Review suitable roles, speak with relevant contacts when available, or submit a few carefully matched applications.

This can reveal whether your current experience is understood and which gaps matter. It does not require resigning or treating every recruiter message as an offer.

Comparing value inside your current company with value to another employer helps explain why an internal reputation may not automatically translate into an external application.

Use the feedback to refine the decision rather than assuming the market owes you a particular result.

Set conditions for staying and conditions for moving

You might stay while completing an approved project, gaining a useful responsibility, or preserving flexibility during a demanding period of life.

You might reconsider if the development opportunity disappears, the work becomes less sustainable, or a genuinely better-fitting role becomes available.

Write those conditions down. They give you a reason to revisit the decision without turning every difficult week into a job-search emergency.

Build options while you decide

A clearer specialty and a small body of relevant evidence can improve your ability to evaluate future roles, even when you are not ready to leave today.

The Six-Figure IAM Career Playbook helps develop that direction through role selection, practical projects, and positioning. The $9 guide is a planning tool, not a forecast of what changing jobs will earn you.

The cost of staying is worth examining. So is the value of what you already have. Make the comparison deliberately.
