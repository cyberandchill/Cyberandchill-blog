What I'd Check Before Leaving an Office IT Job for a Remote Identity Role

Before trading an office IT job for a remote identity role, verify the responsibilities and the working arrangement separately.

An IAM title doesn't establish what you'll own. “Remote” doesn't establish where you may work, when you must be available, or how much travel is expected.

Use the offer process to clarify both. You can value flexibility while still checking whether the team has a realistic plan to help you succeed in the work.

Confirm what you will own in the first months

Ask for examples of the responsibilities a new hire is expected to handle independently.

Will you administer an existing platform, investigate sign-in problems, build integrations, or coordinate access processes? Which tasks require escalation, and who provides support?

Compare the answer with the evidence you actually bring.

A role can stretch you productively, but it shouldn't depend on a capability you've accidentally implied rather than genuinely developed. Clear expectations protect both sides from discovering the mismatch after you start.

Clarify what remote means for this employer

Ask about approved work locations, required travel, core hours, and any office attendance expectations.

Don't assume a remote role permits work from any country, every time zone, or a personal device. The employer may have operational, security, or employment restrictions.

Get important conditions clarified through the normal written offer and policy process.

The goal is not to negotiate every detail into perfection. It is to understand the arrangement you're actually agreeing to rather than the one you imagined from the posting.

Understand support and on-call obligations

Ask how incidents are covered, how often the role participates, and what response expectations apply.

Also ask about planned maintenance windows and customer-facing availability if relevant. A remote job can still have a demanding schedule.

Use representative examples rather than asking only whether the team values work-life balance. “How is after-hours identity support covered?” is easier to answer concretely.

See questions that reveal the on-call model for a more detailed version of that conversation.

Ask how the team brings a new hire into the systems

You don't need confidential architecture details before accepting. You do need a credible explanation of how you'll learn and gain appropriate access.

Ask:

  • Training: What does the initial learning period involve?
  • Permissions: How are access and change authority approved?
  • Documentation: Where are system responsibilities and procedures recorded?
  • Practice: Is there a supported test environment?
  • Escalation: Who helps with an unfamiliar integration or decision?

“You'll figure it out” can mean genuine autonomy, inadequate support, or something in between. Ask for a concrete example of how a recent team member became productive.

The goal isn't to eliminate every unfamiliar task. It's to understand whether independent work comes with the information, permissions, and support required to do it responsibly.

Compare the complete employment tradeoff

Review guaranteed compensation separately from conditional components and understand the benefits and employment terms.

Then compare the role's learning potential, stability, schedule, and location constraints with your current job. Seek qualified advice for legal, tax, or complex compensation questions rather than relying on a recruiting summary.

You may decide the new opportunity is clearly better. You may also discover that the current role gives you a stronger base while you build a missing identity skill.

The remote label shouldn't make the rest of the comparison disappear.

Prepare for the transition without pretending you're already experienced

Before starting, organize your relevant notes, refresh the concepts the role actually uses, and identify questions for onboarding.

Do not request unauthorized system access or use company information for personal preparation. A fictional lab can help you practice the general concepts safely.

Be clear about what you know and what you'll need to learn in the employer's environment.

The Six-Figure IAM Career Playbook helps build that foundation through role selection, practical labs, and positioning. The $9 guide supports a more informed move, not a guarantee that every remote identity role will improve your life.

Choose the job after you understand the work, the support, and the conditions. Then the flexibility becomes a real benefit rather than an assumption you have to renegotiate later.
