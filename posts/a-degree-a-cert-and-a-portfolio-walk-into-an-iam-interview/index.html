A Degree, a Cert, and a Portfolio Walk Into an IAM Interview

A degree, certification, and portfolio provide different kinds of evidence in an IAM application.

Education describes a learning path. A credential identifies an assessed qualification. A portfolio lets someone inspect selected work and reasoning.

None answers every hiring question. Compare the role's actual requirements with the evidence you have, then decide which missing piece deserves attention. You don't need to turn the three options into a contest with one universal winner.

Start with the employer's actual requirements

Read the qualification section and the responsibilities separately.

An employer may have an explicit education or credential requirement. You should not assume a portfolio overrides it. Another role may emphasize experience and practical capability more heavily.

Use the posting and hiring conversation to understand the specific situation rather than turning one person's success story into a universal rule.

The application needs to satisfy relevant gates while also explaining why you can perform the work. Those are connected, but they are not identical questions.

Use education to establish the foundation accurately

List your degree or educational status truthfully and make relevant areas of study clear where they help the application.

Do not assume the reader will infer hands-on identity experience from a broad program title alone.

If coursework included a relevant project, describe the project as coursework and explain what you actually did. Keep it separate from paid professional responsibility.

The education section is evidence of learning. It becomes more useful when the rest of the application shows how that learning connects to the role's practical requirements.

Use certifications to state an accurate, relevant qualification

List credentials according to their real status:

  • Earned and current: Describe them accurately and emphasize relevant ones.
  • Expired: Don't imply the credential is currently active.
  • In progress: Don't present preparation as completed certification.

Check any program requirements that affect how the credential may be described.

A certification can communicate assessed knowledge or meet an explicit requirement. It doesn't establish that you have independently performed every covered task in production.

Be prepared to explain the concepts and connect them to actual examples. Where your experience comes from a lab, say so.

The credential should support the application, not replace the explanation of your capability. Its relevance becomes clearer when the reader can see how the knowledge, professional experience, and project evidence fit the work being hired for.

Use the portfolio to make selected work inspectable

A project should state the requirement, your contribution, the tests, and the limitations.

For IAM, a small directory access, SSO, or lifecycle scenario can make your reasoning visible. Label it clearly as a lab and use fictional data.

The portfolio does not need to compete with a degree by claiming to be “better education.” It serves a different purpose: showing how you approached a bounded problem.

Use honest lab evidence to keep the distinction between demonstrated practice and production ownership clear.

Let the interview connect the evidence

An interviewer may ask how the credentialed knowledge applies to a problem, what you learned in the project, or how your professional experience changes your approach.

Prepare to explain those connections. If a project exposed a gap in your understanding, discuss how you investigated it. If a certification helped structure the learning, explain that without claiming it guarantees job readiness.

A coherent account of learning, practice, and real responsibility is more useful than arguing that one category of evidence makes the others irrelevant.

Choose the next investment based on the missing evidence

You may need a qualification, a practical example, a clearer resume, or more relevant experience. The right next step depends on what is missing for the role you want.

Don't buy another credential solely because it is easy to count. Don't build another lab solely because you dislike studying for an explicit requirement.
