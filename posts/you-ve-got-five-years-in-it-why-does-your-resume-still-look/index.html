You've Got Five Years in IT. Why Does Your Resume Still Look Entry-Level?

Five years in IT should give you more to explain than the same task list you had in your first year. But your resume may not show that progress.

Check whether the work stayed the same or the wording hides how it changed. Those are different problems.

If you've taken on more difficult investigations, broader responsibilities, or better judgment, make that visible without inventing seniority or a job title you never held.

Compare your first year with your current responsibilities

Write two short descriptions without looking at your resume.

In the first, describe what you needed help with when you started. In the second, describe the work you can now handle, explain, or coordinate more independently.

Look for changes in judgment, not just more tools. Perhaps you now recognize recurring access problems, prepare better escalations, train colleagues, or identify when a request needs additional approval.

Those differences are the raw material for showing progression.

Replace participation statements with the work you completed

“Attended meetings,” “worked with users,” and “used Active Directory” describe participation. They don't explain your contribution.

Compare them with specific outputs:

Vague activity More informative direction
Attended project meetings Clarified an access requirement and documented the decision
Supported user issues Investigated a sign-in failure using relevant evidence
Used Active Directory Completed approved account or group changes and verified the result
Helped the team Documented a repeatable troubleshooting process

Use only examples that match your actual work. The point isn't to make every sentence sound like leadership or architecture.

It is to let a reader see what you were trusted to do, what you produced, and what required your judgment. That is more informative than listing years beside product names.

Show the decisions you were trusted to make

Mid-career evidence often involves knowing when to act, when to investigate, and when to escalate.

Choose an example where the request was incomplete, the symptom was misleading, or the first obvious fix would not have addressed the cause.

Explain what you checked and why. Keep the scope of your authority clear.

A candidate who can describe sound judgment within a defined role may present a stronger case than one who uses senior-sounding verbs without a specific example behind them.

Make relevant scope concrete without exposing private details

Scope can include the type of environment, the variety of responsibilities, or the teams involved.

You don't need exact user counts or financial impact when you cannot verify or disclose them. A truthful description of supporting several applications or coordinating with a separate identity team can still be useful.

Never invent metrics to make the resume look more experienced. Don't include customer names or internal identifiers without permission.

Precision comes from explaining the work, not from adding numbers to every line.

Check whether the target role can see the connection

A strong resume for one job can look unfocused for another.

For an IAM application, bring relevant account, access, authentication, lifecycle, and troubleshooting examples forward. Reduce unrelated detail that competes with those examples.

If the evidence still doesn't support the target, identify a real development gap. A new lab or approved project may be needed before better wording can solve the problem.

Use a focused cut of a long IT resume when the challenge is too much history rather than too little experience.

Tell a progression story you can defend

The reader should understand what you did, how your responsibility developed, and why the next role is a reasonable move.

That story may include a period of repetition or a job with limited advancement. You don't need to hide it. Show what you learned, what you can now demonstrate, and what you're deliberately building next.

Years belong in the employment dates. Growth belongs in the responsibilities and examples. Make sure the resume shows both when both are true.
