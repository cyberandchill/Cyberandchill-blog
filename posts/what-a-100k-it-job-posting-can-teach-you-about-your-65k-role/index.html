What a $100K IT Job Posting Can Teach You About Your $65K Role

A higher-paying IT job posting can show which responsibilities differ from your current work, even before you're ready to apply.

Use the $100K-versus-$65K comparison as a question to investigate, not a promise that one skill produces a fixed salary increase.

Compare the duties, context, and employment conditions. You may discover a real capability gap, a positioning problem, or similar work valued differently by another employer. Those findings call for different next steps.

Compare the expected work before comparing certifications

Read what the person must do, then compare it with your current responsibility.

Look for differences such as:

  • Deeper or more independent troubleshooting.
  • Building and supporting application integrations.
  • Owning a defined process or service.
  • Making design decisions and explaining tradeoffs.
  • Similar tasks in a different organizational context.

A credential list alone can hide these distinctions. The same technology names may appear in jobs with very different levels of authority and accountability.

Ask what evidence would establish your ability to handle the changed responsibility. Is the gap education, practical experience, clearer examples, or simply a job search you haven't started?

That diagnosis makes the posting useful. A salary number without the work behind it is not enough to decide what to study next.

Identify the work you already perform

Mark the requirements that connect to real examples from your current role.

A general support title may include approved access changes, account lifecycle tasks, or authentication investigations. Describe the actual scope rather than assuming the broader title makes those responsibilities irrelevant.

Be equally careful not to overclaim. Using a platform for one task does not establish readiness for every role involving that platform. Write down the specific example you would use in an interview to support each match.

Separate missing capability from missing wording

Sometimes the resume is the weak link: relevant work exists, but the application describes it vaguely.

Other times, the gap is real. You may have supported a sign-in process without ever integrating an application or testing the authorization behavior.

Fix the right problem. A wording change cannot replace missing experience. Another certification cannot automatically solve a resume that hides relevant responsibility. A focused lab may help demonstrate a concept, while an approved work opportunity may be needed for deeper operational ownership.

Check what the compensation comparison leaves out

The advertised number may reflect location, experience level, variable compensation, schedule, travel, or other conditions.

Read the complete posting and clarify the actual arrangement during the process. Do not assume the role is comparable to your current one simply because both are in IT.

You are evaluating an opportunity, not proving that your current employer owes the difference based on one listing.

A useful comparison includes the work, conditions, and evidence required—not just the amount that made you open the page.

Choose one gap with a practical next action

Suppose the role repeatedly emphasizes access lifecycle work and your strongest experience is account creation.

A useful next step could be a fictional joiner-mover-leaver project that includes approval, removal of obsolete access, and verification. Keep it labeled as a lab and explain its limits.

Use an experience inventory against a job posting to turn the comparison into a small plan.

The objective is to improve your candidacy for relevant work, not to recreate every requirement from a long listing before you allow yourself to apply.

Use more than one posting to test the direction

Compare several suitable opportunities so one unusual listing does not define your entire roadmap.

Look for recurring responsibilities and meaningful differences. Then make a career action: revise the evidence, build the focused project, seek an approved development opportunity, or apply where the match is already credible.

Keep adjusting based on real feedback rather than a salary screenshot.
