That 3% IT Raise Looks Different When You Compare Outside Offers

Before comparing a 3% raise with an outside offer, compare the actual job and compensation—not just the percentage.

A new offer can reveal options your current employer doesn't provide. It can also include responsibilities, travel, or availability requirements you don't want.

Use the numbers to start the comparison, then examine the work and conditions. Leaving isn't automatically right, and staying isn't automatically a failure to recognize your value.

Compare the base salaries on the same basis

Suppose a fictional employee earns a $75,000 base salary.

A 3% increase adds $2,250, making the new base $77,250. A hypothetical outside offer of $90,000 base would be $12,750 higher than that revised amount.

Those figures are arithmetic, not a statement about current IAM salaries or what any reader should expect to receive.

The comparison is useful because it uses the same compensation category. Don't compare a guaranteed base with another employer's maximum possible compensation and call the difference certain.

Understand what sits outside the headline number

Ask about variable pay, benefits, equity terms, travel expectations, and other conditions that affect the overall offer.

You may need professional advice for tax, legal, or complex compensation questions. Don't infer the value of every component from the recruiting summary.

For an initial career comparison, identify what is guaranteed, what is conditional, and what remains unclear.

A larger advertised number can be attractive while still requiring careful questions before you decide.

Clarify what the higher-paying role expects you to own

An offer pays for a particular job under particular conditions. Find out what those conditions involve during the first months.

For an identity role, distinguish:

  • Operations: Maintaining and supporting an existing service.
  • Implementation: Building or changing integrations.
  • Incident ownership: Investigating failures and coordinating recovery.
  • Governance: Organizing access decisions, reviews, and evidence.

A role can combine these areas. Ask about their actual balance and the support available, rather than inferring the work from the title.

Broader responsibility can be an attractive development opportunity or an unwanted workload. Compare it with your goals and current capability.

The higher number matters, but it isn't a price tag for doing exactly what you already do. Make sure you understand the different agreement you're considering.

Give flexibility and stability an explicit place

Remote arrangements, schedule control, team support, and the health of the working environment may matter substantially to you.

Write them into the comparison instead of assuming the larger salary makes them irrelevant. Also avoid assuming that staying is risk-free simply because the current environment is familiar.

You don't need to assign a dollar value to every preference. You do need to know which tradeoffs are acceptable and which are not.

Use a fuller comparison of pay and remote work when location freedom is part of the decision.

Use market information without turning it into a threat

Researching suitable roles and considering offers can help you understand your options.

If you discuss progression with your current employer, be specific about responsibilities, contribution, and the organization's process. Don't make threats you don't intend to act on or assume an outside number guarantees a matching internal adjustment.

The employer may have constraints. You may decide those constraints no longer fit your goals.

A clear decision is more useful than trying to prove that one side has objectively valued you incorrectly.

Build options before you need an urgent exit

The ability to compare opportunities depends partly on having relevant evidence and a coherent application.

Choose a target role, identify the gap, and build a practical example while you still have time to evaluate offers carefully. Don't wait until frustration forces every decision into an emergency.

The Six-Figure IAM Career Playbook connects those steps through IAM role selection, three labs, and a 90-day plan. The $9 guide supports building options; it does not promise a particular offer or salary increase.

A percentage raise is one piece of information. Compare the complete opportunities, then choose the trade you can justify for your own career and life.
