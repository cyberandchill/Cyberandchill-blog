Before You Renew That Old IT Cert, Ask These Four Questions

Before renewing an old certification, check whether active status supports work you do now or a role you genuinely plan to pursue.

An expiry email isn't automatically a career emergency. Renewal may be necessary, useful, or a lower priority than another piece of evidence.

Use these four questions to compare the requirement, relevance, current renewal obligations, and alternative use of your time. Neither renewing everything nor letting everything expire is a universal career strategy.

1. Is active status required for something specific?

Start with concrete requirements: your current role, an employer agreement, a contract, or a target job that explicitly requires an active credential.

Don't treat “preferred” and “required” as identical. Don't assume a requirement is optional because you personally consider the exam less useful than experience.

When the wording is unclear, ask the relevant employer or credential provider. You need the actual rule that applies, not a general internet argument about whether certifications matter.

Write down the consequence of allowing it to lapse. A clear requirement can make the decision straightforward.

2. Does the material support your next role?

Review the credential's current scope against the responsibilities you want to perform.

An older networking certification may still support your troubleshooting foundation. A security certification may provide useful terminology but leave your immediate application-integration gap untouched.

The question isn't whether the subject has any value. Almost every IT subject can be useful somewhere. The question is whether refreshing it deserves priority over the skills your target role actually needs.

Compare the renewal work with one saved job posting and one gap you are trying to close.

3. What does renewal actually require now?

Check the provider's current process rather than assuming renewal means repeating the original exam or paying the original price.

For example, Microsoft offers a free online renewal assessment for eligible certifications through its certification renewal process. Eligibility, deadlines, and the credential involved still matter.

Other providers use different rules. Verify the applicable policy before making a cost comparison.

Include preparation time, required activities, deadlines, and any fees in your decision. A small, relevant renewal effort may make sense even when the credential is not the center of your career plan.

4. What would the same effort produce elsewhere?

Compare the next few study sessions with another realistic use of that time.

Option Obstacle it could address
Keep the credential active An actual qualification or employment requirement
Finish a relevant IAM project Missing practical evidence for the target responsibility
Improve applications Experience that suitable employers aren't seeing clearly

Sometimes renewal is the strongest choice because active status is required. Sometimes the project matters more because you meet credential expectations but can't yet explain the work.

You may be able to do both by reducing unrelated study. The purpose is to compare priorities, not create a rule that exams and practical work are enemies.

Choose based on which action removes a meaningful obstacle in your current plan, rather than reacting only to the deadline in the renewal notice.

Record the decision so you don't revisit it every week

Write a short note with the credential, deadline, relevant requirement, expected effort, and your decision.

If you renew, connect the preparation to a practical example so the work reinforces understanding. If you don't, update your resume and profiles accurately. Don't continue presenting an expired credential as active.

Keep evidence of historical achievement separate from claims about current status. The appropriate wording depends on the provider's rules and the context in which you're listing it.

The approach to choosing which certifications belong on a mid-career resume can help with the presentation afterward.

Keep the career plan bigger than the renewal calendar

A certification deadline is an administrative event. It shouldn't become the only thing determining what you learn.

Review your target role, current capability, and practical evidence alongside your credentials. That prevents a sequence of renewals from replacing a deliberate career move.

Renew the credential when it serves that plan. Let the decision be specific enough that you can explain why it was worth doing—or why another use of your time mattered more.
