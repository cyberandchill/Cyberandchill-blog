What to Cut From a Ten-Year IT Resume Before an IAM Application

For an IAM application, a ten-year resume should make relevant experience easy to find without giving every old duty equal space.

Ask whether each detail helps explain your ability to perform this role—not whether the work was ever important.

That lets you shorten older material without distorting employment history. Keep foundational experience when you can explain its connection, and use the space to make your strongest responsibilities and evidence more visible.

Remove repeated duties before removing useful history

Start by marking duties that appear under several jobs with little change: answered tickets, supported users, maintained systems, and resolved technical problems.

A reader doesn't need five versions of the same general statement.

Keep the strongest, most relevant example and use the recovered space for responsibility, scope, or a specific problem. Earlier roles can often be summarized more briefly while remaining visible in your employment timeline.

Don't remove a meaningful progression simply because an arbitrary template says every resume must fit a particular page count.

Keep tool names only when you can explain their relevance

For each technology in the skills section, imagine an interviewer pointing to it.

Could you explain:

  • Your actual involvement with the tool.
  • The environment or task where you used it.
  • A problem or responsibility it supported.
  • Its relevance to the IAM position you're pursuing?

Remove items that are both weakly supported and irrelevant. Retain useful foundational knowledge when you can show the connection, even if the posting uses newer terminology.

The difference between relevant experience and keyword stuffing is a useful check before adding a product name simply because the advertisement contains it.

The goal isn't the shortest possible skills section. It is a list whose claims survive follow-up questions and help the reader understand your capability, rather than making the important evidence harder to notice.

Shorten old detail that no longer supports your direction

An early desktop-support role may deserve a few lines, not the largest section on the page.

Keep details that establish transferable capability: account administration, access troubleshooting, controlled changes, or documentation. Reduce unrelated equipment lists and routine tasks that don't add anything beyond the role title.

Use the same test for older certifications. Don't imply an expired credential is active, and don't let an extensive certification list crowd out stronger evidence of relevant work.

You are editing for the present application, not erasing the path that got you here.

Replace broad claims with a supported example

Consider a line such as “Experienced IT professional with excellent problem-solving skills.”

That may be accurate, but it doesn't help a reader evaluate you. A concrete description of troubleshooting account access across two systems can do more, provided it reflects work you actually performed.

Don't invent impact numbers to make the example sound impressive. State the problem, your contribution, and the result you can support.

When you contributed to a team effort, describe your part. “Assisted with” can be more credible than an inflated ownership claim that falls apart in an interview.

Keep lab projects clearly separate from employment

A relevant lab can fill an evidence gap, but it should not be inserted into a former job as if it happened there.

Create a clearly labeled projects section. Explain the scenario, tools, tests, and what you demonstrated. Use a link for supporting detail rather than trying to fit the whole walkthrough into the resume.

This lets you show current learning while keeping your professional record honest.

A ten-year background plus a focused new project can tell a coherent story without turning the project into invented production experience.

Read the edited version against one job description

After cutting, check the first half of the resume. Can a reader identify the responsibilities you have handled that connect to the role?

Then check what might raise questions: unexplained gaps, unclear dates, overstated skills, or a summary that promises more than the bullets support.

Ask someone to explain your intended career move after a quick read. Their answer reveals whether your editing improved the story.
