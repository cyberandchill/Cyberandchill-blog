Why I Wrote a $9 IAM Playbook Instead of Another Giant Course

I wrote a $9 IAM playbook to help an IT professional answer “What should I do next?”

Someone with support, networking, or systems experience may need a role target, an honest skill-gap check, and a useful first project more than another enormous course catalog.

A short guide doesn't replace documentation or practice. It can organize the next decision so learning serves a career move instead of expanding indefinitely without a clear destination.

The intended reader has IT experience but no clear next step

Picture a support technician who already handles accounts, access requests, and login issues.

They want a better-paying role but keep collecting advice about different exams, platforms, and specialties. Each recommendation sounds reasonable; together, they could consume years.

The immediate questions are smaller:

  • Which IAM role fits the experience they already have?
  • Which requirement do they genuinely need to learn?
  • What could a useful first lab demonstrate?
  • What should they do on Monday?

Those are the decisions I wanted the book to help with.

It isn't designed to teach every identity product or guarantee a salary. It gives someone who already knows some IT a more deliberate starting point, so they can spend the next period developing and demonstrating relevant capability rather than assembling a bigger collection of recommendations.

My own path made practical direction important to me

I started out delivering pizzas. Unpaid work installing network switches helped me get a foothold in IT. My path later included network support, security software, and identity work.

That history is relevant because I did not begin with a perfect cybersecurity roadmap. I had to make decisions from the opportunities and knowledge available at each stage.

It does not make my exact route a template everyone should follow. Your location, experience, constraints, and available jobs may be different.

The transferable lesson is to connect learning with work you actually want to become capable of doing.

A small guide should still produce concrete decisions

A useful career guide should leave you with more than encouragement.

After reading, you should be better able to explain which IAM lane you are exploring, why it fits your background, and what evidence you need next.

For example, “I am learning cybersecurity” is difficult to act on. “I am investigating identity administration roles and need a project that demonstrates group-based access” gives you a starting point.

The book covers five IAM lanes, three practical lab scenarios, certification decisions, positioning, and a 90-day execution plan. Those pieces belong together because choosing a project without considering the job can send you in the wrong direction.

You still need documentation and practice

A compact playbook cannot replace the documentation for the product you install. It cannot anticipate every licensing condition, configuration screen, or troubleshooting result.

You still need to build, test, read, and verify.

That is not a hidden extra requirement. It is the work the guide is meant to help you choose. When you reach an implementation question, use the relevant vendor documentation and keep your experiments inside an isolated environment you control.

The limits of the two-certs-and-three-labs formula are worth understanding before treating any learning plan as a hiring guarantee.

The price does not replace a clear description

A $9 product can still waste your time if it solves the wrong problem.

This book is a reasonable fit when you already have some IT experience and want a structured way to explore identity work. It is not a complete beginner's IT education, personal coaching, or a promise of employment within ninety days.

You may already know exactly which role you want and have strong practical evidence. In that case, your next useful action may be applying, interviewing, or working through a specific technical gap rather than buying another resource.

I would rather make that distinction clear than persuade the wrong reader.

Judge the book by what you do after reading

The useful outcome is a saved job posting, an honest experience inventory, and a project you can explain when it is finished.

You can begin that process without buying anything. Find a role, underline the responsibilities you understand, and circle one gap you can investigate safely.

The Six-Figure IAM Career Playbook puts the selection process, lab ideas, and application plan in one $9 guide. Buy it for that structure, not because a short book can do the career work for you.
