The $70K IT Job That Feels Safe Until You Look Up

A comfortable $70K IT job can be a reasonable choice. The question is whether you're choosing it with an understanding of your other options.

Keep your career options current even when you don't intend to leave. That means knowing which skills transfer, what outside roles require, and what conditions would make a move worthwhile.

You don't need to manufacture dissatisfaction with a job that suits your life. A periodic check simply helps you avoid becoming dependent on one environment without noticing.

Comfort is not the problem

Predictability has value. You may have family responsibilities, health needs, or other priorities that make an aggressive career move unattractive right now. You don't owe the internet a new job title every year.

What you do owe yourself is an honest description of the decision.

Are you staying because the role still fits? Because you are building toward something? Or because investigating another path feels uncomfortable?

You can respect the stability you have while noticing that the work no longer develops the capability you want.

No dramatic resignation is required to answer that question.

Check which skills would transfer to another employer

Imagine your current job ended. What could you explain to a hiring team that knows nothing about your employer?

Check three areas:

  1. Responsibilities: Can you describe the work without relying on internal job names or acronyms?
  2. Technical understanding: Can you explain how the systems function beyond your company's procedures?
  3. Examples: Can you discuss a recent investigation, implementation, or improvement without sharing confidential details?

Keep this about capability rather than fear. Company-specific knowledge can be valuable, and maintaining it may be an important part of your job.

The issue is whether you also have transferable skills and clear explanations. If your experience is difficult to describe outside one organization's context, choose a small project or learning task that broadens what you can demonstrate.

You can do that while deliberately staying in a job you like.

Run a small career maintenance check

Set aside an occasional block to review roles you might consider.

You aren't committing to an application. You're checking how the work outside your current environment is described.

Compare the responsibilities with your background. Note one skill you already have and one that would require practice.

Refresh a resume example while the details are still clear. Review whether your public profile describes what you actually do.

This is maintenance, not a declaration that your current job is bad.

It keeps the idea of a future move from becoming an enormous project you must start only when circumstances force it.

Build one thing that creates an option

For someone interested in identity, a small lab can provide a contained way to explore.

Choose a question such as how group membership controls a resource or what changes when a fictional employee moves departments.

Finish the exercise, test it, and document the result.

You may discover an interest worth developing. You may decide the work isn't for you. Either result gives you information while preserving the job you currently value.

Don't turn the exploration into a second full-time obligation.

An option is useful when it fits into your life well enough to maintain.

Decide what would make you move

Write the conditions under which another role would be worth considering.

Perhaps it would need to offer relevant responsibility, better schedule control, a workable location, or compensation that justifies the change. Perhaps your current role would remain preferable even if another paid somewhat more.

This prevents every attractive headline from becoming a crisis.

It also makes interviews more useful because you have questions connected to your actual priorities.

A move should solve a problem you care about. Leaving merely because you feel embarrassed by your current salary isn't a sound reason on its own.
