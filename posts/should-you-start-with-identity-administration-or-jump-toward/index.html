Should You Start With Identity Administration or Jump Toward Engineering?

An identity-engineering title may sound like progress, but the better question is whether you're ready for the decisions attached to it.

Compare the responsibility and ambiguity you'd own—not just administrator versus engineer. An administration role with substantial troubleshooting can offer a stronger next step than a design role without the support you need.

Read the work, identify the expectations, and ask how the team handles unfamiliar problems. The goal is a role that develops your next capability without requiring you to pretend you've already demonstrated it.

Look for the decisions inside an administrator role

An identity administrator may handle accounts, groups, application access, policies, and operational issues. The exact scope depends on the employer.

When reading the posting, ask:

  • Exceptions: Does the person diagnose problems or only route them?
  • Service ownership: Are they responsible for keeping a service reliable?
  • Improvement: Can they recommend or implement better procedures?
  • Boundaries: Which decisions require another team's approval or design?

Microsoft's identity administrator overview includes lifecycle and access responsibilities. It illustrates one role description, not a universal definition of the title.

A narrow runbook position and a broad operational role can share the same label. Identify which one you're considering before dismissing administration as too junior—or assuming it will provide the depth you're hoping to gain.

Engineering often asks you to create or change the approach

An engineering-oriented position may expect you to evaluate requirements, design an integration, automate a process, or plan how a change will be supported.

Instead of receiving a complete procedure, you may receive a problem: “This application needs employee sign-in and reliable access removal.”

You then need to ask the missing questions, compare options, define tests, coordinate owners, and explain the tradeoffs.

That is not simply administration performed faster. It is a different level of responsibility for deciding what should be built and how the organization will operate it.

Test your readiness with an incomplete requirement

Give yourself a fictional request: a new team needs access to a reporting application, but nobody has defined the approval process or the role model.

Can you identify the missing information before configuring anything? Can you explain the risks of copying another team's permissions? Can you propose a small test and a rollback approach?

You do not need to solve every enterprise problem independently. You need to know when the problem is underspecified and which people should help resolve it.

A portfolio project can demonstrate some of that reasoning. It cannot substitute for every kind of production experience, and you should describe its limits honestly.

Ask how the team supports the role

A position may be a reasonable stretch when there are experienced colleagues, review processes, and a clear onboarding plan.

The same title can be a poor move when you are expected to become the only identity expert on day one.

Ask who reviews designs, who handles escalations, and what a successful first three months would involve. Request an example of a recent project the position would have owned.

Questions to ask an identity team before accepting a role can help you distinguish a supported progression from a title attached to an unreasonable expectation.

You can also ask what decisions a new hire must make without review. The answer often reveals the practical seniority of the role more clearly than a list of years or a broad engineering title.

Choose the role that produces the next useful capability

An administration role may give you the operational foundation you need: recurring failures, change control, access ownership, and the consequences of configuration decisions.

An engineering role may fit when you already handle those responsibilities and can show relevant design or implementation work.

Neither title guarantees higher pay, better work, or a faster career. Compare the actual responsibilities, compensation, support, and working conditions.
