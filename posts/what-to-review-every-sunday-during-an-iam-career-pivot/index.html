What to Review Every Sunday During an IAM Career Pivot

A Sunday IAM career review should finish with one clear decision for the coming week.

Look at what changed in your capability, what remains blocked, and whether the work still serves the role you want. Don't turn the review into another hour of criticizing yourself for missed study time.

Keep it short and concrete enough that Monday begins differently. The value is a better next action, not a more elaborate record of how busy the week felt.

Start with what you can now explain or demonstrate

Look at the week through outputs rather than effort alone.

You might have completed an access test, understood a sign-in error, rewritten a relevant resume example, or identified a job description that fits your background better.

Write one or two sentences about the change. “Watched three videos” tells you what you consumed. “Can explain why this user can authenticate but lacks the application role” tells you what you learned.

Both may belong in your notes, but the second should guide the next step.

Separate unfinished work from blocked work

An unfinished project may simply need another session. A blocked project may need a decision, a prerequisite, or a smaller scope.

For example, a feature unavailable in your test environment is not a personal discipline failure. You may need to change the exercise, check current requirements, or demonstrate the underlying concept another way.

Write the blocker precisely. “The lab doesn't work” is too broad. “I cannot test this policy with the current account and feature access” gives you something to investigate.

Do not keep assigning yourself the same impossible task without changing the conditions.

Check whether the week's work still matches the target

Compare what you did with the responsibilities in your saved job posting.

Ask:

  1. Was the new tool necessary for the intended capability?
  2. Did extra infrastructure help prove a requirement?
  3. Is a more important gap being postponed because another topic is more enjoyable?
  4. What should continue, stop, or narrow next week?

You can keep both hobbies and career projects. Label them honestly rather than requiring every interesting experiment to be a hiring advantage.

The one-role, one-gap, one-proof approach is useful when the plan spreads across unrelated subjects.

This check isn't a reason to discard everything whenever an objective becomes difficult. It helps preserve the work that serves the direction and separate optional exploration from the next action most likely to improve your evidence.

Review application feedback without overreacting

If you applied for roles, record what happened and what you actually know.

No response doesn't prove a specific weakness. An interview question you couldn't answer may reveal a clearer gap. A recruiter explaining an essential requirement gives you information you can use.

Separate observations from interpretations. “No interviews from these applications” is an observation. “I need a different certification” is only one possible explanation.

Look for a testable adjustment rather than rebuilding the entire career plan every weekend.

Choose one main outcome for the next week

Set an outcome small enough to finish alongside your job.

For example: complete the denied-access test, publish a sanitized project summary, or revise the resume for a particular type of identity role.

Then identify the first action and put it into an available work block. Avoid a list of ten priorities that leaves you choosing from scratch each evening.

Keep a minimum version for a difficult week. That might be documenting the current test result and identifying the next check, rather than completing the entire project.

Stop the review when the decision is made

You don't need a sophisticated dashboard to make progress. A dated note with evidence, blocker, feedback, and next outcome is enough.

Over several weeks, those notes show whether your preparation is producing new capability or simply repeating the same intentions.

They also make it easier to restart after an interruption because you can see the exact next step.
