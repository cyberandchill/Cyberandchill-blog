What Recruiters Mean When They Ask for 'Hands-On Entra Experience'

When a recruiter asks for “hands-on Entra experience,” ask which tasks and level of independence the role requires.

The phrase could mean established account procedures, sign-in investigations, application configuration, or ownership of complex changes. Those are not equivalent claims.

Use a concrete example from your background to make the comparison. Keep production and lab work distinguishable so the conversation identifies a real fit or gap instead of relying on the product name alone.

Clarify the responsibilities expected in the first months

Ask: “Which Entra responsibilities are most important in the first few months?”

Then narrow the answer:

  • Users and groups?
  • Application integrations?
  • Authentication troubleshooting?
  • Access policies?
  • Governance or another defined area?

Follow with the expected independence: operating an established procedure, assisting an owner, or making and validating decisions yourself.

This avoids two opposite mistakes. You won't dismiss relevant experience merely because it covers one area, and you won't assume that familiarity with one area qualifies you for every other part of the platform.

The role's tasks should determine the comparison. Once they're clear, describe the closest genuine example, identify its scope, and say which remaining responsibility you haven't yet performed. That is more informative than answering the broad phrase with an equally broad yes or no.

Clarify the expected level of independence

Performing a task from an approved procedure is different from designing the procedure or diagnosing unfamiliar failures independently.

Ask what the new employee is expected to own and what support is available.

For your own evidence, distinguish work you performed, work you assisted with, and work you only studied. A lab can show practical understanding, but it should be labeled as a lab.

This makes the discussion more useful than answering “yes” to the broad experience question and hoping nobody asks what that means.

Use a concrete example instead of a tool list

Choose one relevant task and explain the requirement, your action, and the verification.

For example, you might describe an approved account change, a sign-in investigation, or a test application integration—whichever reflects your actual experience.

Name the relevant object and scope. Explain what you checked and what remained outside your responsibility.

A short, specific account gives the recruiter or hiring team something to evaluate. “Used Entra, Azure, Microsoft 365, and security tools” leaves too much interpretation to the reader.

Keep professional and lab experience distinguishable

You can say: “My professional experience includes account administration. My application-integration example is an independent lab with fictional users and documented tests.”

That is a coherent answer, not a confession that the lab has no value.

The employer can decide whether the combination fits the role. You avoid creating an expectation of production ownership that the interview will later expose as unsupported.

Use clear project labeling in the resume so the same distinction is visible before the conversation begins.

Identify the gap the conversation reveals

If the role requires a capability you haven't demonstrated, ask enough to understand the scope.

You may be able to build a focused project, seek an approved development opportunity, or target a nearby role first. You may also discover that the employer needs experience you cannot responsibly substitute with a short lab.

Both findings are useful. The goal is not to talk your way around every gap. It is to understand which gap matters and choose a realistic response.

Prepare a small evidence bank for future conversations

Keep a few safe, concise examples organized by responsibility rather than product name.

For each, note the problem, your scope, action, result, and whether it came from employment or a lab. Avoid retaining confidential details in personal notes.

This makes applications and interviews easier because you are selecting relevant evidence rather than improvising a broader claim each time someone asks about the platform.
