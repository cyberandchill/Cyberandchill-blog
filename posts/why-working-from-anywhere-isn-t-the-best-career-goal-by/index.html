Why Working From Anywhere Isn't the Best Career Goal by Itself

Working from anywhere is a location preference, not a complete career plan.

You still need work you can do well, conditions you can sustain, and skills you can explain to another employer. A desirable destination won't make constant availability or an unsuitable job disappear.

Treat location freedom as one requirement alongside pay, schedule, authorization, and development. Then evaluate whether a particular opportunity provides the combination you actually want.

Separate the place you work from the way you work

A remote role tells you something about location. It doesn't automatically tell you about autonomy.

You could work from home and still have a rigid schedule, constant interruptions, or an on-call rotation that shapes your personal life. You could also have useful flexibility within a role that requires occasional planned travel.

Write down the conditions you actually care about. Include working hours, response expectations, travel, time off, and the ability to plan your day.

“Remote” is a starting label. The details determine whether the arrangement fits your life.

Confirm where you are authorized to work

Don't build a lifestyle plan around assumptions about an employer's location policy.

Ask which locations are permitted, whether temporary travel requires approval, and what process applies before you work somewhere different. Get important conditions clarified through the employer's normal channels.

This is not a problem to solve by hiding your location or bypassing controls.

A useful question is: “How do employees request permission to work temporarily from another location, and what restrictions should I know about?” That invites a concrete answer rather than a vague promise of flexibility.

Ask what capability the next year of work would develop

A convenient arrangement can still leave your career stagnant. Examine what you would learn or own.

Would the role let you:

  • Investigate more difficult failures?
  • Build or support integrations?
  • Develop stronger access-governance reasoning?
  • Improve a process and verify its effect?
  • Explain a broader responsibility than you can today?

Or would you mostly repeat a narrow task with little opportunity to develop?

Choosing stability for a period can be reasonable. Make the choice deliberately and decide whether development needs to happen separately.

A location arrangement becomes harder to replace when it depends on one employer and experience you can't communicate elsewhere. Relevant capability doesn't guarantee another remote offer, but it gives your next search something concrete to build on if circumstances change.

Compare the complete package instead of one benefit

Imagine two fictional offers with similar pay. One permits home-based work but includes frequent unplanned evening coverage. The other has a predictable schedule and occasional travel agreed well in advance.

Neither is automatically better. A parent, frequent traveler, or someone protecting study time might value those conditions differently.

Compare the actual tradeoffs with your priorities. Avoid treating a single attractive feature as permission to ignore every other part of the job. The same discipline applies when a larger salary is the feature getting your attention.

Build freedom into the way you develop skills

You don't have to leave a good job immediately to explore a specialty.

Use a bounded learning schedule, fictional lab scenarios, and suitable applications to test whether identity work fits your experience. Keep employer information and personal projects separate, and respect applicable workplace policies.

A practical starting point is building IAM skills while keeping your current remote job. That approach lets you investigate a direction without making a lifestyle change before you understand the work.

The aim is to create options, not to make your current life harder merely to feel ambitious.

Define the career you want in complete sentences

Try this instead of “I want to work from anywhere”:

“I want a role with approved location flexibility, predictable working expectations, technical work I can build on, and compensation that supports my priorities.”

Now you can evaluate an opportunity against something concrete.

Choose the work and conditions together. A better backdrop for your laptop won't compensate for a job that doesn't fit your life.
