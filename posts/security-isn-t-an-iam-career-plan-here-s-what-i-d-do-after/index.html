Security+ Isn't an IAM Career Plan. Here's What I'd Do After It.

Security+ gives you a foundation in security concepts. It doesn't decide which job you should pursue next.

If you've earned it and want to move into Identity and Access Management (IAM), don't choose your next certification just because someone online recommended it.

Use the next few weeks to connect what you've learned to an IAM role and a practical skill you can demonstrate.

Here's where I'd start.

First, pick a real identity job

Look for a position such as IAM Analyst or Microsoft Entra ID Administrator.

Read three job postings and compare the responsibilities. You might see requests to manage users, troubleshoot access, support single sign-on, configure MFA, or administer identity policies.

Those responsibilities tell you more about what to study than a generic list of “top cybersecurity certs.”

For each requirement, ask:

  • Have I performed this at work?
  • Can I explain it without looking at notes?
  • Could I demonstrate it in a small lab?

Your next learning target should address a meaningful gap, not simply add a new badge to LinkedIn.

Learn the difference between signing in and getting access

Security+ introduces authentication and authorization. IAM requires you to apply the distinction to actual systems.

Imagine an employee who signs in successfully but can't open an application.

Several things could be true:

  • Their account is valid and authentication succeeded.
  • They haven't been assigned the application.
  • Their application role doesn't permit the requested action.
  • An access policy blocks the session.

The right diagnosis depends on the system and the evidence. A password reset is not the universal solution.

Microsoft's authorization overview explains how access decisions differ from establishing identity.

Practice explaining the flow in ordinary language. If you can't identify where the decision happens, that's a useful skill gap to investigate.

Build one lab that turns theory into evidence

Here's a manageable starting exercise:

Goal: Demonstrate that two test accounts can both authenticate but have different permissions.

Create an isolated test environment, a resource, and two fictional users. Grant access through an appropriate group or role to one user, not the other.

Test both accounts. Then change an assignment and verify what happens under the appropriate session conditions.

Save:

  1. The access requirement you were testing.
  2. A small diagram showing identities, groups or roles, and resource.
  3. The expected and observed results.
  4. A few sentences explaining one failure or surprise.

Now you have something to discuss in an interview beyond passing an exam.

If the jobs you're studying focus on SSO instead, use a small sign-in integration. The principle is the same: choose a responsibility, test it, explain it.

Decide whether another certification is actually useful

Once you've investigated the role and built an example, make a more informed decision.

If your gap is… Consider…
Microsoft identity administration Reviewing current SC-300 objectives and practical Entra exercises
A missing hands-on skill Completing a targeted lab before booking an exam
Weak resume positioning Rewriting accurate examples and applying to suitable roles
An employer's explicit certification requirement Evaluating that specific credential and its renewal obligations

A certification can be useful. It just needs to serve the role you're targeting.

Also check the current official exam objectives. Certification content and names can change.

Give the next month a finish line

Instead of “keep studying cybersecurity,” aim for three concrete outputs:

  • One IAM job target with requirements you understand.
  • One small project with working evidence and an explanation.
  • One resume update or application based on those skills.

You might discover that your best next step is SC-300. You might discover you already qualify for some roles and should apply now.
